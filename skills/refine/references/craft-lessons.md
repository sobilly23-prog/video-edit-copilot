# Craft lessons

These came out of real review rounds on a long personal travel film. Each lesson was a correction the editor (a person)
made, or a rule they stated, that improved the cut. They're defaults for long-form video: the user's taste profile overrides any of them. For short-form, and for types
learned from the user's examples, start from `video-types.md`.
The reason is given with each lesson, so it can be applied with judgement rather than mechanically.

## Shots and timing

- **Cut when the action finishes, never mid-gesture.** The pour lands in the cup, the hand takes the receipt, the
  camera move settles on the sign, the first bite happens. Then trim the dead tail.
  *Why:* a shot cut mid-action "feels chopped off", even if it's long enough.
- **What you came for gets time; getting there stays quick.** Arrivals, walks and transit run about 2–6 s. The
  subject (food, coffee, the making of something, a striking room) runs as long as the shot stays good: until the
  camera moves away, someone talks, or the take ends. 4–6 s is the *minimum* for a subject, not the target.
  *Why:* viewers came for the subject. Short holds on it feel rushed, and long walks feel slow.
- **When unsure how long to hold an interesting shot, don't guess small.** Offer options (as on a caption picks page).
- **A long, steady process take can become a timelapse:** normal speed for the start, about 5× through the middle
  (muted, with music under it), normal speed for the result. Not 20×: too fast reads as a gimmick. Handheld walks
  don't suit it.
- **Drop posing and waiting shots of companions,** and trim reaction shots to their core.
- **One best take.** No duplicate angles of the same moment.
- **Shaky starts and ends go,** usually the first and last 0.5–1 s. A shaky shot worth keeping can be stabilised
  outside the app if the app has no stabiliser.

## Order inside a place: tell it the way the viewer would live it

Capture order doesn't matter; experience order does.
- **Arrival:** the street → the building → the sign → the door.
- **A meal:** drinks → the table reveal (a wide, or the dishes arriving) → for each dish, its close-up (with its name)
  and then its bite → a wide of everyone eating → leave.
- **A café:** the counter → the drinks being made → the drinks → anything bought on the way out (a bag of beans goes
  last).
- **Preparation runs forwards:** empty plate → filling → finished.
- **A view out of a window belongs with the other window shots,** not between two shots of the counter.
- **Before handing over a meal, look at a one-frame-per-shot contact sheet** and check the order reads like a story.

## Between places

- **Bridge every move from one place to the next** so the viewer knows where you're going: a short plain line on the
  travel shot before the next place's name card ("Across the road to the night market", "Back in the old town for
  dinner", "A taxi to dinner").
- **No travel footage?** Look for unused walking shots first. Failing that, put the line on the new place's first shot
  and move its name card a few seconds later. Never put a line and a card on screen together.
- **A street shot too short for its line** can be lengthened to about 4–5 s.
- **Where a place ends with no exit shot,** a plain 1 s dissolve is the better transition.
- **Short atmosphere shots between scenes** (a view through a window, a street detail) give the viewer a breath.

## Openings, chapters, endings

- **Open with highlights from this video only,** about 20–30 s of short clips, ending on the calmest wide shot, with
  the title over it. Don't reuse shots that appear later.
- **Chapter cards** (for example "Day 2") on a short black card, with a small subtitle naming the area.
- **End on a song's natural ending,** with a fade to black on the last shot, and the audio fading with it.

## Finding footage

- **Before saying a shot doesn't exist, look for it.** People remember footage the copilot hasn't used yet: check the
  unused clips around that time, and the phone. Match by what's in the picture, since camera and phone clocks differ.

## Privacy

- **Leave out people who aren't with you** where they're identifiable, especially children. If they can't be left
  out, ask the user what they want done.

## Working with the person

- **Never undo their edits;** learn from them (see the `learn` skill).
- **Offer options instead of asking open questions.** People find picking far easier than describing taste.
- **Anything that sounds like their opinion is a draft for them to approve.**
- **Say what you checked and what you didn't.**
